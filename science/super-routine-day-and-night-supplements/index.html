"Day and night supplements" simply means splitting a supplement routine by time of day, because a few nutrients work better, or are safer, at a particular time. Most of the evidence is about practical things: taking fat-soluble vitamins with a meal, keeping a sleep hormone away from the morning commute, and spacing nutrients that can compete. This page explains how the Super Routine day and night sachets apply those rules, and where timing matters less than marketing suggests.

Why the Day sachet goes after breakfast

Vitamins D and K need a meal. According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, the presence of fat in the gut enhances vitamin D absorption, and vitamin K is absorbed in the small intestine along with dietary fat. Breakfast is the most regular meal for most people, which makes the routine easy to keep. Our 600 IU of vitamin D3 matches the ICMR-NIN adult requirement for Indians and is well below the 4,000 IU upper limit.

Zinc is easier on a full stomach. Nausea is a known effect of too much zinc, and many people find even normal doses unsettling on an empty stomach. Our 12 mg is under a third of the 40 mg upper limit.

B12 has no best time, but it is part of daily energy metabolism and sits naturally with the morning routine. The NIH notes that absorption of large oral doses is low (about 2% at 500 mcg), which is why supplements usually contain far more than the 2.2 mcg daily requirement, and no upper limit has been set.

Why the Night sachet goes after dinner

Melatonin only makes sense at night. It is the hormone that tells the body clock it is night. Mayo Clinic lists daytime drowsiness among its common side effects and advises not driving or using machinery within five hours of a dose, which rules out the morning entirely.

Ashwagandha fits the evening. The NIH summarises a 2021 meta-analysis of five studies (372 adults) that found a small but significant benefit for sleep at 250–600 mg a day over 6–12 weeks, with larger effects at 600 mg. Drowsiness is one of its reported side effects. Our 300 mg is within that studied range.

Magnesium and omega-3 are flexible. The NIH does not tie magnesium absorption to time of day, and omega-3 can be taken whenever suits you. We put them in the Night sachet because the evening meal is a natural fit and it keeps magnesium away from the morning zinc.

Why zinc and magnesium are in different sachets

To be accurate: at normal doses most people can take zinc and magnesium together. The interaction the NIH describes involves very high zinc intakes of about 142 mg a day, far above our 12 mg. We split them anyway because minerals are absorbed in the same part of the gut, and putting them in different sachets removes the question completely. Who should not take Super Routine day and night supplements

  • Anyone pregnant or breastfeeding, or under 18.
  • People taking warfarin or other blood thinners: vitamin K changes warfarin's effect, and melatonin may interact with medicines that slow clotting.
  • People with epilepsy or taking seizure medicines, diabetes or blood pressure medicines, immunosuppressants or birth control: Mayo Clinic lists these as possible melatonin interactions.
  • People taking thyroid medicine, sedatives, or diabetes or blood pressure medicines, or with a liver or thyroid condition: ashwagandha may interact or carry risks.
  • People on statins (high vitamin D intakes may reduce the effect of some), metformin or long-term acid-reducing medicines (which lower B12), or quinolone and tetracycline antibiotics (take them at least 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after zinc or magnesium).
  • People with kidney problems (magnesium toxicity risk rises) or kidney, liver or prostate conditions (WebMD advises discussing shilajit first).

Day and night sachets vs one multivitamin

A once-a-day multivitamin cannot place melatonin at bedtime or keep minerals apart, and many do not contain omega-3 or ashwagandha. FAQ

What are day and night supplements?

They split a supplement routine by time of day: nutrients that absorb best with a meal or support the day go in the morning, and nutrients for the evening, such as melatonin, go at night.

Can I take the Super Routine day and night sachets at the same time?

No. The Night sachet contains melatonin, which can cause drowsiness, so it belongs after dinner. Take the Day sachet after breakfast.

What if I forget the Day sachet?

Take it with your next meal the same day, not at bedtime, and never take two Day sachets to catch up.

Is Super Routine a multivitamin?

No. It is two sachets with eight specific nutrients at labelled doses, split between day and night. It does not contain iron, calcium or folate.
