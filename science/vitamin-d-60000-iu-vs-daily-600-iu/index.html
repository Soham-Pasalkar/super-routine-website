Vitamin D 60,000 IU weekly vs daily 600 IU: which is right for you?

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 27 Oct 2026

60,000 IU is a treatment dose, usually taken once a week for 6–12 weeks to correct a measured deficiency. 600 IU a day is the adult maintenance requirement (ICMR-NIN and NIH). A weekly 60,000 IU sachet averages about 8,570 IU a day, over twice the 4,000 IU upper limit, so it is not a long-term dose.

Adult requirement600 IU/day
Adult upper limit4,000 IU/day
60,000 IU weekly≈ 8,571 IU/day
Adequate blood level20 ng/mL or more

Vitamin D3 60,000 IU sachets and capsules are among the most searched supplements in India, and many people take them without a test, often for months. This guide explains what the two doses are for, using the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS), the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) and Indian clinical studies that used the 60,000 IU regimen.

The daily averages are simple arithmetic (60,000 ÷ 7 and 60,000 ÷ 30). 1 mcg of vitamin D equals 40 IU.

What 60,000 IU weekly is used for in India

In Indian clinical studies, 60,000 IU appears as a short course for people whose blood test shows low vitamin D, not as an everyday supplement. Examples:

  • Women with vitamin D below 20 ng/mL received 60,000 IU weekly for 8 weeks in a Jabalpur study.
  • Patients at Tata Memorial Centre with levels of 20 ng/mL or less received 60,000 IU weekly for 8 weeks, followed by 800 IU a day as maintenance.
  • Children with deficiency received 60,000 IU weekly for 6 weeks at BHU Varanasi. Most reached sufficiency, but only 23% of those who did were still sufficient 3 months after stopping. The authors stress the need for regular monitoring and maintenance.

The pattern is consistent: a correction course, a re-test, then a lower maintenance dose. A 60,000 IU sachet every week with no end date is not part of that pattern.

Weekly or daily: does it matter?

Both can raise blood levels. In a 2026 randomised study of 50 vitamin D-deficient adults with long-bone fractures, 60,000 IU weekly for 12 weeks raised blood levels more than 1,000 IU daily (32.8 vs 27.3 ng/mL at 12 weeks), and both were well tolerated. That was a supervised treatment setting with baseline levels below 30 ng/mL.

For healthy people, regular modest doses look sensible. A 2021 meta-analysis of 43 trials found a small overall reduction in acute respiratory infections with vitamin D; the protective effect was seen with daily doses of 400–1,000 IU, although the formal test for a difference between daily and less frequent dosing was not significant. Very large, infrequent doses can backfire: in a trial of 2,256 women aged 70 or older, a single 500,000 IU dose each year increased falls by 15% and fractures by 26% compared with placebo.

How much is too much?

The NIH uses blood 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) levels to judge status:

Blood 25(OH)DWhat the NIH says
Below 12 ng/mL (30 nmol/L)At risk of deficiency
20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) or moreGenerally adequate for bone and overall health in healthy people
Above 50 ng/mL (125 nmol/L)Linked to potential adverse effects
Above 150 ng/mL (375 nmol/L)Typical of vitamin D toxicity

Toxicity raises blood calcium. This is not only theoretical in India: at PGIMER Chandigarh, vitamin D intoxication caused 8.7% of the cases of sustained high blood calcium among hospitalised adults.

People also respond differently. In a Delhi study, children with obesity given 60,000 IU weekly for five doses had a 25% smaller rise in blood levels than children of normal weight, while some normal-weight children briefly went above the normal range. This is why the right dose depends on a test, not on a pack size.

Whatever the dose, take it with a meal: the NIH notes that fat in the gut enhances vitamin D absorption. Orlistat can lower vitamin D absorption, and vitamin D can interact with some statins. For timing details, see the best time to take vitamin D3 and B12.

FAQ

Is 60,000 IU vitamin D safe to take every week?

Only as a short course prescribed after a blood test. Taken weekly it averages about 8,570 IU a day, which is above the 4,000 IU adult upper limit, and long-term use without monitoring can raise blood calcium.

How long should I take vitamin D 60,000 IU?

Indian studies typically used 6 to 12 weeks, followed by a re-test and a lower maintenance dose. Follow your doctor's plan rather than continuing indefinitely.

Is 600 IU of vitamin D a day enough?

600 IU is the daily requirement for most adults (800 IU over 70) and is meant to maintain normal levels. It is not designed to correct a diagnosed deficiency.

Can I take daily vitamin D after a 60,000 IU course?

That is the usual pattern in studies: a correction course followed by a daily maintenance dose. Ask your doctor which dose suits your blood level.
