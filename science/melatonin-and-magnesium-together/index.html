Melatonin and magnesium together for sleep: is it safe, and does it help?

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 27 Oct 2026

Yes, most healthy adults can take melatonin and magnesium together: the NIH fact sheets list no interaction between them, and they work differently. Melatonin signals night to your body clock; magnesium supports normal nerve and muscle function. Keep supplemental magnesium under 350 mg a day, take melatonin 30–60 minutes before bed, and do not drive afterwards.

Melatonin and magnesium are two of the most common evening supplements, and many sleep products combine them. This guide explains what each one does, what the research on the pair shows (less than marketing suggests), and how to take them safely. It draws on the NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH), the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS), Mayo Clinic and published trials.

Is it safe to take melatonin and magnesium together?

For most healthy adults, yes. The NIH fact sheets on magnesium and melatonin do not list an interaction between the two, and they act on different systems. The risks of the pair are the risks of each one on its own, so the safety rules below still apply.

The bigger risk is doubling up. Many "sleep blends" already contain melatonin, and some antacids and laxatives contain magnesium. Check every label so you are not taking two melatonin products, and count all supplemental magnesium towards the 350 mg limit. See supplements not to take together.

What the research shows for sleep

Melatonin

The NCCIH says there is not enough strong evidence to recommend melatonin for chronic insomnia. It is better supported for sleep timing: in people with delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, a 2016 review found melatonin shortened the time to fall asleep by about 22 minutes compared with placebo. Reviews also found it may reduce jet lag symptoms after eastward flights. Short-term use appears safe for most people; long-term safety is not known.

Magnesium

The evidence for magnesium alone is weaker. A 2026 systematic review of 12 randomised trials found inconsistent results and rated the certainty of evidence low to very low. Its conclusion: current evidence does not support oral magnesium as a routine treatment for insomnia, though some adults may notice modest benefits. The American Cancer Society likewise describes the results as mixed, and notes that magnesium glycinate is a common choice because it is easily absorbed and gentle on the stomach.

Melatonin and magnesium together

We found one placebo-controlled trial of the combination. In 2011, Rondanelli and colleagues gave 43 long-term care residents in Italy (average age about 78) with primary insomnia either a supplement of 5 mg melatonin, 225 mg magnesium and 11.25 mg zinc, or placebo, one hour before bed for 8 weeks. The supplement group had better sleep quality scores and longer total sleep time.

The limits matter. It was a small study in older adults in care, the supplement also contained zinc, and the design cannot show how much each ingredient contributed. It suggests the combination is reasonable to study further; it does not prove that the pair works better than melatonin alone for younger adults.

How to take melatonin and magnesium together

  • Magnesium: take it with dinner or in the evening. A meal reduces stomach upset. Glycinate and other chelated forms are less likely to loosen stools than oxide.
  • Melatonin: take it 30–60 minutes before your planned bedtime. The Rondanelli trial used one hour before bed.
  • Keep the dose modest. Mayo Clinic notes that over-the-counter products vary widely in how much melatonin they contain; the NCCIH reports that a 2017 study found most of 31 products did not match their label. Choose a product that states an exact dose.
  • Do not drive or use machinery within five hours of taking melatonin (Mayo Clinic).
  • Use both alongside good sleep habits: a regular wake time, a dark room and less screen time late at night.

Who should not take melatonin and magnesium together

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women: melatonin has not been well studied in pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • People with epilepsy or taking blood thinners: the NCCIH says they need medical supervision to take melatonin.
  • People with dementia: the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends against melatonin for them.
  • People taking diabetes, blood pressure, seizure or immunosuppressant medicines, or birth control pills: Mayo Clinic lists these as possible melatonin interactions.
  • People with kidney disease: the risk of magnesium build-up is higher when the kidneys clear it less well.
  • Antibiotics and bone medicines: take tetracyclines or quinolones (such as doxycycline or ciprofloxacin) at least 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after magnesium, and keep bisphosphonates such as alendronate 2 hours apart from it.

Persistent trouble sleeping deserves a doctor's visit. Supplements are not a substitute for diagnosing the cause.

FAQ

Can I take magnesium and melatonin together every night?

Many adults do, and the NIH lists no interaction between them. Melatonin appears safe short term, but long-term safety is not known, so if you need help sleeping for more than a few weeks, see a doctor.

Which is better for sleep, melatonin or magnesium?

They do different things. Melatonin has better evidence for sleep timing and jet lag; evidence for magnesium is mixed and of low certainty. Neither is recommended as a routine treatment for chronic insomnia.

When should I take magnesium and melatonin?

Take magnesium with dinner or in the evening, and melatonin 30 to 60 minutes before bed. Do not drive or use machinery within five hours of melatonin.

Can I take magnesium glycinate, melatonin and ashwagandha together?

They are often combined in the evening, but ashwagandha and melatonin can both cause drowsiness. Avoid alcohol and sedatives with them, and ask your doctor first if you take any regular medicine or are pregnant.
