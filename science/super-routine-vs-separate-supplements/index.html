How to work out the cost of 8 separate supplements

For each product you are considering, take the price you would actually pay and divide by the number of days it lasts at the dose you want:

StepCalculation
1. Servings per packTablets or capsules in the pack ÷ tablets you need per day
2. Cost per dayPack price ÷ days from step 1
3. TotalAdd the cost per day of all eight products
4. MonthlyTotal per day × 30
5. CompareAgainst ₹40 a day (₹1,200 a month)

Two adjustments make the comparison honest. First, if a product's dose is double what you want, you might split it, which halves its cost per day; if it is half, you need two, which doubles it. Second, count the leftovers: buying eight packs at once means a large upfront spend, and packs of different sizes run out at different times.

Doses: reading labels the right way

Matching doses across eight bottles is harder than it looks, mainly because of minerals. A mineral compound is only partly the mineral itself, so a label that says "magnesium glycinate 1000 mg" is the whole compound; the elemental magnesium is about 140 mg. Always compare elemental amounts.

Check the totals against the upper limits too, especially if you already take a multivitamin:

Forms: where separate products differ most

  • Omega-3: the NIH says re-esterified triglycerides (rTG), natural triglycerides and free fatty acids have somewhat higher bioavailability than ethyl esters, although all forms raise blood EPA and DHA.
  • Magnesium: forms that dissolve well in liquid are absorbed more completely, and oxide is among the forms most linked to diarrhoea. Glycinate is gentle on the stomach.
  • Vitamin B12: to be fair, the NIH says there is no evidence that absorption differs by form. We use methylcobalamin, one of the active forms, but cyanocobalamin works too.
  • Ashwagandha: the trials in the NIH summary used specific root extracts such as KSM-66 at 250–600 mg a day; a generic "ashwagandha powder" may not match.

Timing: two sachets vs eight bottles

The other cost of separate supplements is organisation. Some nutrients suit the morning and some the evening: vitamin D and K are fat-soluble and absorb with a meal, melatonin belongs at bedtime, and very high zinc intakes can reduce magnesium absorption, which is one reason we keep zinc and magnesium in different sachets. With eight bottles you build that schedule yourself; with Super Routine it is one sachet after breakfast and one after dinner. Read how the Super Routine day and night sachets work for the full reasoning.

When separate supplements are the better choice

  • You need only one or two nutrients. If a blood test shows only low vitamin D or B12, a single product is cheaper and simpler.
  • Your doctor prescribed a different dose, such as a weekly high-dose vitamin D sachet or iron.
  • You cannot take one of our ingredients, for example melatonin because of epilepsy or blood thinners, ashwagandha because of pregnancy or a thyroid condition, or vitamin K because you take warfarin.
  • You are vegetarian and want no fish oil. Our omega-3 is from fish; algal oil is the vegetarian alternative.

If none of these apply and you would otherwise buy most of the eight, the two-sachet routine is usually simpler, and the price comparison above tells you whether it is cheaper for you. Compare also our guide to supplements not to take together.
