Omega-3 for vegetarians in India: flaxseed, chia or algal oil?

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 29 Oct 2026

Vegetarians get omega-3 as ALA from flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts and soybean or canola oil, but the body converts less than 15% of ALA into EPA and DHA. Studies show vegetarians have low EPA and DHA levels, and that algal (microalgae) oil, a vegetarian source of DHA, raises them. Fish oil is the other option for those who eat it.

Omega-3 is one of the nutrients vegetarians in India most often ask about, because the best-known source, fish, is off the menu. The answer depends on which omega-3 you mean. This guide explains the three types and what the research shows, using the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS), ICMR-NIN and peer-reviewed studies.

ALA, EPA and DHA: three different omega-3s

Omega-3Main sourcesVegetarian?
ALA (alpha-linolenic acid)Flaxseed (alsi), chia, walnuts, soybean and canola oilsYes
EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid)Fish, fish oil; some algal oilsOnly from algae
DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)Fish, fish oil, algal oilOnly from algae

ALA is an essential fat: you must eat it. EPA and DHA are the long-chain forms found mainly in fish and algae. The body can make EPA and DHA from ALA, but the NIH says this conversion is very limited, at less than 15%, so eating EPA and DHA directly is the only practical way to raise their levels.

Vegetarian ALA sources and how much you need

The NIH Adequate Intake for ALA is 1.6 g a day for men and 1.1 g for women. ICMR-NIN gives a combined target of about 2.2 g of omega-3 fats a day for Indian adults. ALA is easy to get from food:

Food (NIH data)ALA per serving
Flaxseed oil, 1 tablespoon7.26 g
Chia seeds, 1 ounce (28 g)5.06 g
Walnuts, 1 ounce (28 g)2.57 g
Whole flaxseed, 1 tablespoon2.35 g
Canola oil, 1 tablespoon1.28 g
Soybean oil, 1 tablespoon0.92 g

A tablespoon of ground flaxseed in curd or a roti dough covers the ALA requirement. ICMR-NIN also advises limiting visible fat, for example to about 27 g of cooking oil a day on a 2,000 kcal diet, so add seeds rather than extra oil.

Do vegetarians have low EPA and DHA?

Yes, according to the studies we found. The omega-3 index measures EPA + DHA in red blood cells; around 8% is often described as desirable. In the Adventist Health Study-2, people who did not take fish oil had these average levels:

DietOmega-3 indexEPA + DHA intake
Vegan3.7%10 mg/day
Lacto-ovo vegetarian3.9%18 mg/day
Pesco-vegetarian (eats fish)5.7%158 mg/day
Non-vegetarian5.0%117 mg/day

The authors concluded that vegans and vegetarians were well below recommended targets, and that the most reasonable way to raise levels is an algae-derived EPA and DHA supplement. More ALA did not seem to fix this: a 2021 review of 13 randomised trials found that high-dose flaxseed or echium oil did not raise the omega-3 index, while microalgal oil raised it in every study.

Algal oil: the vegetarian source of DHA

Algal oil comes from microalgae, the organisms that make the DHA fish later accumulate. The NIH says algal oil supplements usually provide about 100–300 mg of DHA per serving, some with EPA, and a small study found its DHA as bioavailable as DHA from cooked salmon. In a randomised trial in 104 healthy vegetarians, 0.94 g of DHA a day from microalgae oil for 8 weeks raised the omega-3 index from 4.8% to 8.4%, and 69% reached 8% or more; nobody on placebo did.

When choosing one, check the label for the actual milligrams of DHA and EPA per capsule, not just "1000 mg algal oil", and whether the capsule shell is vegetarian.

Safety, side effects and who should be careful

  • Dose: no upper limit has been set; EFSA and the US FDA consider up to about 5 g a day of EPA + DHA from supplements safe.
  • Side effects are usually mild: an unpleasant taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, stomach upset, diarrhoea or headache.
  • Blood thinners: fish oil may prolong clotting time in people taking warfarin, although 3–6 g a day did not significantly change it in studies. Ask your doctor if you take an anticoagulant.
  • Heart rhythm: 4 g a day for several years slightly increased atrial fibrillation risk in people with or at high risk of heart disease.
  • Pregnancy: pregnant vegetarians should ask their doctor whether they need EPA or DHA.

FAQ

Is flaxseed enough omega-3 for vegetarians?

Flaxseed easily covers ALA, the plant omega-3. But the body converts less than 15% of ALA into EPA and DHA, and trials of high-dose flaxseed oil did not raise the omega-3 index. Algal oil is the vegetarian way to get DHA directly.

Which omega-3 is best for vegetarians?

Algal (microalgae) oil, because it provides DHA, and sometimes EPA, directly. In a trial in vegetarians, 0.94 g of algal DHA a day for 8 weeks raised the omega-3 index from 4.8% to 8.4%.

How much omega-3 should a vegetarian take daily?

The NIH Adequate Intake for ALA is 1.6 g a day for men and 1.1 g for women. No official EPA + DHA target is set for vegetarians; algal oil supplements usually give 100–300 mg of DHA per serving.
