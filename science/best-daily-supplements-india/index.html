Best daily supplements in India: an honest guide

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 27 Oct 2026

The best daily supplements are the ones that fill a real gap in your diet. For many Indian adults that means vitamin D (little sun), vitamin B12 (vegetarians, older adults), folic acid for women who could become pregnant, and iron or calcium only if diet or a blood test shows a need. Food always comes first.

How to choose daily supplements

  1. Look at your diet first. Vegetarian? Little dairy? Rarely in the sun? Those answers point to specific nutrients.
  2. Test before you take iron or high-dose vitamin D. Both can cause harm in excess.
  3. Add up every product. The NIH warns that people who combine a multivitamin with other supplements and fortified foods can exceed upper limits.
  4. Pick products that print exact doses, so you can check them against the limits above.

1. Vitamin D

ICMR-NIN sets the adult requirement at 600 IU a day. The NIH lists people with limited sun exposure, darker skin and older adults among those most likely to be low. Take it with a meal that contains some fat. Keep total intake under 4,000 IU a day unless your doctor prescribes a treatment dose; excess raises blood calcium and can cause nausea, weakness and kidney stones. Timing is covered in the best time to take vitamin D3 and B12.

2. Vitamin B12

B12 occurs naturally only in animal foods, so vegetarians have a higher risk of deficiency, says the NIH. People over 50 are advised to get B12 mainly from fortified foods or supplements, and metformin and acid-reducing medicines can lower B12 levels. The ICMR-NIN requirement is 2.2 mcg a day; supplements contain far more because only a small fraction of a large oral dose is absorbed. No upper limit has been set.

3. Folic acid (for women who could become pregnant)

The CDC recommends that all women capable of becoming pregnant get 400 mcg of folic acid daily, because taking it before and in early pregnancy lowers the chance of neural tube defects. If you are planning a pregnancy, talk to your doctor about a prenatal supplement.

4. Iron (only if you need it)

Women with heavy periods, pregnant women and vegetarians are more likely to run short of iron, according to the NIH. But iron is not a "take it just in case" supplement. The adult upper limit is 45 mg a day, high doses cause constipation and nausea, and accidental iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6, so store it out of reach. Iron reduces absorption of levothyroxine, and calcium may reduce iron absorption, so take them at different times. Vitamin C improves absorption of iron from plant foods.

5. Calcium (if your diet is low in dairy)

ICMR-NIN sets the adult requirement at 1,000 mg a day. If you eat milk, curd, paneer or ragi daily, you may not need a supplement. If you do, the NIH says absorption is best in doses of 500 mg or less, so split larger amounts, and take calcium carbonate with food. Keep calcium carbonate 4 hours away from levothyroxine.

6. Omega-3 (if you rarely eat fish)

There is no official requirement for EPA and DHA, the omega-3s in fish. If you eat little fish, a fish oil or, for vegetarians, an algal oil supplement is one option; the NIH notes algal oils usually provide about 100–300 mg of DHA. Side effects are usually mild (fishy taste, heartburn). People on warfarin should have their INR monitored.

Do you need a daily multivitamin?

For healthy adults, the evidence is modest. The NIH summarises the US Preventive Services Task Force view: there is insufficient evidence to judge the balance of benefits and harms of multivitamins for preventing heart disease or cancer. A multivitamin can still be a convenient way to fill gaps, especially for vegetarians and older adults, but check that it does not double up on vitamin D, zinc or iron you already take. FAQ

What are the best daily supplements to take?

The ones that fill a real gap. For many Indian adults that means vitamin D, vitamin B12 for vegetarians and older adults, and folic acid for women who could become pregnant. Iron and calcium should be taken only if your diet or a blood test shows a need.

Is it OK to take supplements every day?

Yes, if total intake from all products stays below upper limits, such as 4,000 IU of vitamin D, 40 mg of zinc and 45 mg of iron a day. Check with your doctor if you take regular medicines.

Should I take a multivitamin daily?

It can fill gaps, but for healthy adults the evidence that multivitamins prevent heart disease or cancer is insufficient. Focus on the nutrients your diet lacks and avoid doubling up across products.

Which supplements should vegetarians take daily?

Vitamin B12 is the most important, because it occurs naturally only in animal foods. Vegetarians may also need to watch iron, zinc and omega-3 (algal oil is a plant-based DHA source).
