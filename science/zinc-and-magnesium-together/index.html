Can you take zinc and magnesium together?

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 29 Oct 2026

Yes. For most healthy adults, zinc and magnesium can be taken together at normal doses. The interaction that has been measured involves very high zinc intakes of about 142 mg a day, more than three times the 40 mg upper limit. Keep zinc under 40 mg, supplemental magnesium under 350 mg, and space both from certain antibiotics.

Why people worry about zinc and magnesium together

Zinc and magnesium are both minerals absorbed in the small intestine, so it is reasonable to ask whether they compete. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements gives a clear threshold: very high doses of zinc from supplements (142 mg a day) might interfere with magnesium absorption. Typical supplements contain 8–25 mg of zinc, nowhere near that level.

The two are also given together in research. In a 12-week randomised, placebo-controlled trial, 55 adults took 250 mg of magnesium and 30 mg of elemental zinc together every day, and the authors reported no adverse effects from the combination.

What zinc and magnesium each do

They are often sold together, but they do different jobs.

  • Zinc is a co-factor for hundreds of enzymes. It contributes to normal immune function, protein and DNA synthesis, wound healing and cell division. Whole grains, beans and nuts contain phytates, which bind zinc in the gut and reduce how much is absorbed, a point worth knowing for many Indian vegetarian diets.
  • Magnesium is involved in more than 300 enzyme systems. It contributes to normal muscle and nerve function, protein synthesis and energy production.

Because their roles do not overlap, there is no reason to choose one over the other. The question is only how much of each, and when.

Stay within the daily upper limits for both, counting every product you take.

Two details are easy to miss. The magnesium upper limit counts only supplements and medicines, not magnesium from food. And on a label, "1000 mg magnesium glycinate" is the whole compound: the elemental magnesium is about 140 mg. Always compare elemental amounts.

Watch for hidden zinc

Most people who exceed the zinc limit do it by stacking products: a multivitamin, an immunity tablet and a separate zinc supplement. Some nasal sprays, homeopathic products and denture creams also contain zinc. Long-term intakes of 50 mg of zinc a day or more can inhibit copper absorption, which is a separate reason to keep total zinc under 40 mg. Read more in 7 supplements you should not take together.

  • Zinc: can cause stomach upset and nausea, so many people take it after a meal. Keep it apart from iron supplements of 25 mg or more, which reduce zinc absorption.
  • Magnesium: absorption does not depend on time of day. Many people take it in the evening as part of a wind-down routine. Forms that dissolve well, such as citrate, chloride and lactate, tend to be absorbed better than oxide. Glycinate is gentle on the stomach.

Should you split them anyway?

You do not have to, but splitting has practical advantages. Who should be careful

  • Antibiotics (quinolones such as ciprofloxacin, tetracyclines such as doxycycline): take the antibiotic at least 2 hours before, or 4–6 hours after, zinc or magnesium.
  • Penicillamine: take zinc at least 1 hour apart from it.
  • Bisphosphonates (such as alendronate): separate from magnesium by at least 2 hours.
  • Diuretics: thiazide diuretics increase zinc loss in urine, and loop and thiazide diuretics increase magnesium loss. Potassium-sparing diuretics reduce magnesium excretion. Ask your doctor before adding either mineral.
  • Long-term proton pump inhibitors (usually more than a year) can lower magnesium levels.
  • If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take any regular medicine, check with your doctor or pharmacist first.

FAQ

Is it safe to take zinc and magnesium every day?

For most healthy adults, yes, if total zinc stays under 40 mg a day and magnesium from supplements stays under 350 mg a day. Check with your doctor if you have a medical condition or take regular medicines.

Can I take zinc and magnesium at night?

Yes. Magnesium is often taken in the evening. Some people find zinc unsettles the stomach, so if that happens, take zinc after breakfast instead.

What should you not take with zinc?

Iron supplements of 25 mg or more, quinolone and tetracycline antibiotics, and penicillamine all need spacing from zinc. Long-term doses of 50 mg or more can lower copper levels.

Does zinc block magnesium absorption?

Only at very high doses. The NIH notes that about 142 mg of zinc a day might interfere with magnesium absorption. Normal supplement doses have not been shown to do this.
