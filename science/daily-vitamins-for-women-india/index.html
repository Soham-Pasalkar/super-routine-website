Daily vitamins for women in India: what matters most

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 27 Oct 2026

For most women in India the nutrients that matter most are iron (needs are highest during the menstruating years), folic acid if you could become pregnant, vitamin D and calcium for bones, and vitamin B12 if you are vegetarian. Test before taking iron, keep iron and calcium apart, and stay under each upper limit.

The RDAs are totals from food and supplements. ICMR-NIN uses a reference body weight of 55 kg for Indian women. Pregnancy and breastfeeding change these needs; follow your doctor's prenatal advice.

Iron: important, but test first

Women need far more iron than men during the menstruating years: the NIH RDA is 18 mg for women aged 19–50 versus 8 mg for men. The ICMR-NIN requirement for Indian women is higher still, at 29 mg a day. The NIH lists heavy periods and pregnancy as the main risk factors for deficiency, and notes that vegetarian diets need about 1.8 times more iron.

Iron is still not a just-in-case supplement. The upper limit is 45 mg a day, and doses at or above that commonly cause nausea and constipation. People with haemochromatosis should avoid iron supplements. Ask for a blood test (haemoglobin and ferritin) before starting, and take iron only as advised.

  • Vitamin C, for example from lemon, amla or guava, improves absorption of plant iron.
  • Take iron and calcium supplements at different times of day.
  • Levothyroxine (thyroid medicine) should not be taken within 4 hours of iron.
  • Acid-reducing medicines (PPIs) lower iron absorption.

Folic acid: for any woman who could become pregnant

The NIH states that women of childbearing age should get 400 mcg a day of folic acid from supplements or fortified foods, on top of folate from food. The US Preventive Services Task Force advises starting 400–800 mcg a day at least one month before conception and continuing through the first 2–3 months of pregnancy. Keep folic acid from supplements and fortified foods under 1,000 mcg a day unless a doctor prescribes more. If you take antiepileptic medicines or methotrexate, check with your doctor before taking folate.

Vitamin D and calcium: for bones

Vitamin D contributes to normal calcium absorption and bones. The adult requirement is 600 IU a day; stay under 4,000 IU unless a doctor prescribes a treatment course (see vitamin D 60,000 IU vs daily 600 IU).

For calcium, food is the best starting point: dairy foods such as milk, curd and paneer. If you supplement, absorption is highest in doses of 500 mg or less, so split larger amounts. Calcium carbonate is absorbed better with food; calcium citrate can be taken without food. The NIH notes that higher supplemental calcium might increase the risk of kidney stones, and that calcium should be kept 4 hours from levothyroxine and 2 hours from quinolone antibiotics. The NIH RDA rises to 1,200 mg for women over 50.

Vitamin B12: essential for vegetarians

B12 is found naturally in animal foods and is poorly represented in plants, so vegetarian and vegan women are at higher risk of low levels. Dairy and eggs help, but many vegetarians benefit from a supplement. The ICMR-NIN requirement is 2.2 mcg a day.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Needs change in pregnancy (the NIH iron RDA rises to 27 mg), and prenatal supplements should be chosen with your doctor. Some popular "women's wellness" herbs are not suitable: the NIH says ashwagandha may not be safe during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and it may affect thyroid function.

FAQ

What vitamins should a woman take daily in India?

Only those that fill a gap. For many women that means vitamin D (little sun), vitamin B12 (vegetarians), folic acid if pregnancy is possible, and iron or calcium if diet or a blood test shows a need.

Should women take iron every day?

Not without a reason. Iron needs are higher during the menstruating years, but get a blood test first and take iron only as advised. The adult upper limit is 45 mg a day.

Can I take iron and calcium together?

It is better to take them at different times of day, because calcium may reduce iron absorption.

Do I need folic acid if I am not pregnant?

The NIH advises 400 mcg a day of folic acid for all women who could become pregnant, because it matters in the earliest weeks, often before a pregnancy is known.
