Best time to take vitamin D3 and B12, and can you take them together?

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 26 Oct 2026

Take vitamin D3 with a meal that contains some fat, because fat helps absorption; for most people that is breakfast or their main meal. Vitamin B12 can be taken any time, with or without food. You can take vitamin D3 and B12 together: the NIH lists no interaction, so one dose after breakfast works well.

Vitamin D3 and B12 are two of the supplements Indian adults are most often advised to take, and they are commonly prescribed together. Their timing rules are different, though: one depends on food, the other does not. This guide explains both, using the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS), the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) and published research.

Best time to take vitamin D3: with your meal, not on an empty stomach

Vitamin D is fat-soluble. The NIH states that the presence of fat in the gut enhances vitamin D absorption, although some is absorbed without it. So the rule is about the meal, not the clock: take vitamin D3 with breakfast, lunch or dinner, as long as the meal contains some fat (milk, curd, eggs, nuts, ghee or oil in cooking).

One often-quoted study supports taking it with your largest meal. In 2010, Mulligan and Licata asked 17 patients at a US bone clinic, who were not responding to vitamin D treatment, to take their usual dose with the largest meal of the day. After 2–3 months their blood vitamin D levels were on average about 50% higher. It was a small study without a control group, so treat it as a useful hint rather than proof, but it fits the fat-absorption mechanism.

In practice, the best meal is the one you eat every day. For many people that is breakfast, which makes the dose easy to remember.

Best time to take vitamin B12: whenever you will remember it

B12 in food is bound to protein and needs stomach acid to release it. The NIH explains that B12 in supplements is already in free form, so it does not need that step, which means you can take a B12 supplement with or without food. The NIH fact sheet does not recommend any time of day.

The morning is popular simply because it is easy to build into a routine. Large oral doses are standard because absorption through the normal route is limited: the NIH says only about 2% of a 500 mcg dose and 1.3% of a 1,000 mcg dose is absorbed, since the intrinsic factor pathway is saturated at about 1–2 mcg. No upper limit has been set. The NIH also states there is no evidence that absorption differs between supplement forms such as methylcobalamin and cyanocobalamin.

Can you take vitamin D3 and B12 together?

Yes. The NIH fact sheets for vitamin D and vitamin B12 list no interaction between them, and they are absorbed by different routes. Taking them together after breakfast covers both rules: the meal helps vitamin D, and B12 does not mind either way. Combining them also means one habit instead of two, which matters more for results than the exact hour.

Morning or night?

For both vitamins, the evidence we found is about food, not time of day. If your main meal is dinner, taking vitamin D3 then is fine. The one practical reason to prefer the morning is consistency: a breakfast routine is easier to keep than a late-night one.

Who needs to be careful

  • Vitamin D: keep total intake under 4,000 IU a day unless a doctor prescribes more. Too much raises blood calcium, which can cause nausea, vomiting, muscle weakness and kidney stones. Orlistat, statins, steroids and thiazide diuretics interact with vitamin D. If you were prescribed a weekly high-dose sachet, follow your doctor's schedule.
  • Vitamin K2 with D3: no upper limit is set for vitamin K, but it can interact seriously with warfarin. Anyone on warfarin should not start K2 without their doctor.
  • Vitamin B12: metformin and proton pump inhibitors can lower B12 levels. Vegetarians and older adults are at higher risk of low B12, because natural sources are animal foods and absorption from food falls with age.

If you think you may be low in either vitamin, ask your doctor for a blood test rather than guessing a dose.

FAQ

Can I take vitamin D3 and B12 together?

Yes. The NIH lists no interaction between them. Take them together after a meal that contains some fat, which helps vitamin D absorption; B12 absorbs with or without food.

Should vitamin B12 be taken on an empty stomach?

It does not need to be. B12 in supplements is already in free form, so it does not depend on stomach acid to be released from food. Take it whenever you will remember it.

Is it better to take vitamin D in the morning or at night?

Absorption depends on fat in the meal, not on the time. Take it with whichever meal you eat most consistently; for many people that is breakfast.

Should I take vitamin D3 with K2?

Many products pair them. Vitamin K has no upper limit, but it interacts with warfarin, so anyone on warfarin should speak to their doctor first.
