Daily supplements for men in India: an honest day and night stack

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 27 Oct 2026

Most men in India do not need a long list of pills. Worth considering: vitamin D if you get little sun, vitamin B12 if you are vegetarian, and magnesium, zinc or omega-3 if your diet is short. Take vitamin D, B12 and zinc with breakfast, and magnesium in the evening. Skip iron unless a test shows you need it.

Most "men's multivitamin" pages are product lists. This guide starts from what Indian men actually need, using the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) requirements for Indians and the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) for safety limits and interactions. It then sorts the useful nutrients into a simple morning and evening routine. Food comes first; supplements fill the gaps.

The RDAs are totals from food and supplements. ICMR-NIN uses a reference body weight of 65 kg for Indian men.

The morning stack

Vitamin D. The NIH lists limited sun exposure, darker skin, older age and obesity among the factors that raise the risk of low vitamin D, and these apply to many office-based men in Indian cities. Take it with breakfast or another meal. Stay under 4,000 IU a day unless a doctor prescribes a treatment course; excess raises blood calcium and can cause nausea, weakness, excessive thirst and kidney stones. See the best time to take vitamin D3 and B12.

Vitamin B12. B12 comes naturally only from animal foods, so vegetarian men are at higher risk, as are men taking metformin or acid-reducing medicines. Oral absorption of large doses is low (about 1.3% of a 1,000 mcg dose), which is why supplements contain far more than the 2.2 mcg RDA. No upper limit has been set.

Zinc. Zinc contributes to normal immune function. Men need more than women (17 mg vs 13 mg a day per ICMR-NIN), and vegetarians absorb less because of phytates. Keep total zinc under 40 mg a day: about 50 mg or more for weeks can lower copper status. Take quinolone or tetracycline antibiotics at least 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after zinc.

The evening stack

Magnesium. It contributes to normal muscle and nervous system function. Keep supplemental magnesium under 350 mg a day of elemental magnesium; high doses cause diarrhoea. Read the label carefully, because the compound weight is not the magnesium content (see elemental magnesium explained).

Omega-3. There is no RDA for EPA and DHA. For men who rarely eat fish, a fish oil (or an algal oil, for vegetarians, which usually gives 100–300 mg DHA) is one option. Take it with a meal to reduce fishy aftertaste. Men on warfarin should have their INR monitored, and very high doses (4 g a day for years) slightly increased atrial fibrillation risk in people with heart disease.

Optional: ashwagandha or melatonin for sleep and stress. Evidence for ashwagandha on stress and sleep is modest and short-term, and NCCIH notes that guidelines do not recommend melatonin for chronic insomnia. If you try either, see magnesium glycinate and ashwagandha together and the best supplements to take at night.

What men usually do not need

  • Iron: it is not a just-in-case supplement. The adult upper limit is 45 mg a day, and iron interacts with zinc, levothyroxine and levodopa. Take it only if a blood test shows a need.
  • Stacks of single products that overlap: a men's multivitamin plus separate zinc and vitamin D can push you over the limits above. Add up every label.
  • "Booster" claims: be wary of any product that promises hormonal or performance results. Supplements support normal nutrition; they are not medicines.

Safety notes for men

  • Prostate: the NIH says ashwagandha might not be safe for men with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, because it may raise testosterone. WebMD advises men with prostate problems, or on testosterone therapy, to talk to a doctor before taking shilajit.
  • Thyroid, diabetes and blood pressure medicines: ashwagandha may interact with them.
  • Melatonin: people with epilepsy and those on blood thinners need medical supervision. Do not drive after taking it.
  • Kidney disease: do not take magnesium supplements without medical advice.

FAQ

What supplements should a man take daily in India?

Only those that fill a gap. For many Indian men that is vitamin D (little sun), vitamin B12 (vegetarians) and possibly magnesium, zinc or omega-3 if the diet is short. Iron only if a test shows a need.

Should men take a multivitamin every day?

It can fill gaps, but check that it does not double up on vitamin D, zinc or iron you already take, and keep every nutrient under its upper limit.

What is the best time for men to take vitamins?

Vitamin D, B12 and zinc after breakfast, because vitamin D absorbs best with a meal. Magnesium and, if used, melatonin in the evening.

How much zinc should a man take daily?

ICMR-NIN sets the Indian men's requirement at 17 mg a day from food and supplements combined. Keep the total under 40 mg a day.
