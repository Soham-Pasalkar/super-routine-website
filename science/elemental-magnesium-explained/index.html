Elemental magnesium explained: how to read magnesium labels in India

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 26 Oct 2026

Elemental magnesium is the weight of the magnesium itself, without the compound it is bound to. "1000 mg magnesium glycinate" gives only about 140 mg elemental magnesium, because glycinate is roughly 14% magnesium. Compare products by elemental magnesium, and keep the total from supplements under 350 mg a day.

Magnesium never comes alone in a tablet or sachet. It is always bound to something else (glycine, citric acid, oxygen, chloride) to make a stable compound. That compound is what is weighed on many labels, which is why two products that both say "500 mg" can contain very different amounts of magnesium. This guide explains the difference and how to compare products sold in India.

What does elemental magnesium mean?

Elemental magnesium is the share of a compound's weight that is magnesium. The rest is the carrier. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes that a product's Supplement Facts panel declares the amount of elemental magnesium, and ConsumerLab explains that magnesium can make up less than half the weight of compounds such as citrate, chloride and glycinate. Recommended intakes and upper limits are always given in elemental magnesium.

Percentages for oxide, carbonate, hydroxide, chloride, lactate, aspartate, sulfate and gluconate come from a 2019 review in Medical Sciences. For citrate, ConsumerLab gives 11.2% for magnesium citrate and 16.2% for trimagnesium dicitrate, both of which may be labelled "magnesium citrate". The glycinate figure is calculated from its formula: magnesium (24.3 g/mol) is about 14% of magnesium bisglycinate (about 172 g/mol).

More elemental magnesium is not the same as more absorbed

Oxide looks best on paper, with 60% magnesium, but the NIH notes that forms which dissolve well in liquid are absorbed more completely, and that the aspartate, citrate, lactate and chloride forms tend to have higher bioavailability than oxide and sulfate. A small glycinate dose may therefore be a better daily top-up than a large oxide one. Absorption also depends on the dose and on your own magnesium status.

How to read a magnesium label in India

Look for which of these two lines the label uses:

  • "Magnesium glycinate 1000 mg" means the compound. Multiply by the percentage above: about 140 mg of magnesium.
  • "Magnesium (as magnesium glycinate) 140 mg" or "elemental magnesium 140 mg" means the magnesium itself. ConsumerLab explains that a line written this way should contain that amount of actual magnesium.

If a label gives only the compound weight and the brand will not tell you the elemental figure, you cannot compare it fairly with other products. Also check the serving size: a "per serving" figure may mean two or three tablets.

The RDA is your total from food and supplements. The 350 mg upper limit applies only to magnesium from supplements and medicines, not food. A supplement is meant to close the gap between what you eat and what you need, not to supply the whole RDA.

Signs you are taking too much

The NIH says high doses of magnesium from supplements or medicines often cause diarrhoea, which can be accompanied by nausea and abdominal cramping. Very large doses of magnesium-containing laxatives and antacids (typically over 5,000 mg a day) have caused toxicity, and the risk rises with impaired kidney function. Count antacids and laxatives that contain magnesium in your daily total.

Medicines to space from magnesium

  • Tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics (doxycycline, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin): take at least 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after magnesium.
  • Oral bisphosphonates (alendronate): keep 2 hours apart.
  • Diuretics and long-term proton pump inhibitors: they can change magnesium levels; ask your doctor.

FAQ

How much elemental magnesium is in 500 mg of magnesium glycinate?

About 70 mg. Magnesium glycinate is roughly 14% magnesium by weight, so 1000 mg gives about 140 mg.

Is 140 mg of elemental magnesium enough?

It is a top-up, not a full day's need. Indian adults need 370–440 mg a day in total (ICMR-NIN), mostly from food. 140 mg stays well under the 350 mg limit for supplements.

Which form of magnesium has the most elemental magnesium?

Magnesium oxide, at about 60%. But the NIH notes that oxide is absorbed less well than citrate, chloride, lactate or aspartate.

Does the 350 mg limit include magnesium from food?

No. The NIH upper limit of 350 mg covers magnesium from supplements and medicines only. Magnesium from food does not count towards it.
