Ashwagandha KSM-66: dose, timing and safety

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 23 Oct 2026

KSM-66 is a full-spectrum ashwagandha extract made from the root only. Human trials have used 250–600 mg a day for stress and sleep, mostly for 8–12 weeks. The Super Routine Night Pack contains 300 mg, taken in the evening. Avoid it if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have thyroid or liver problems, unless your doctor agrees.

What KSM-66 is

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an Ayurvedic herb. KSM-66 is a branded extract made from the root only and standardised to its withanolide content. Generic ashwagandha powders vary, and some include leaf. A standardised extract means each dose is consistent.

What the research says

Several randomised trials found that ashwagandha root extract lowered perceived stress and cortisol compared with placebo. A 2021 meta-analysis found a small but significant improvement in sleep, larger in people with insomnia. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes the limits: trials are small and short, most were run in India, and the extracts differ. We describe ashwagandha as studied for stress and sleep, not as a treatment.

300 mg vs 600 mg

KSM-66 trials on sleep used 250–600 mg a day, and some found larger effects at 600 mg. We chose 300 mg so the Night Pack works alongside melatonin and magnesium, rather than relying on a single high dose. 300 mg is also the lower end of the range an international psychiatry taskforce suggested for anxiety (300–600 mg of root extract).

When to take ashwagandha

Trials gave it once or twice a day, morning or evening, so timing is flexible. We put it in the evening because the sleep studies are a key reason it is in the formula, and it fits a wind-down routine.

Who should avoid it

  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: avoid. Several authorities advise against use.
  • Thyroid conditions or thyroid medicine: ashwagandha can change thyroid hormone levels. Speak to your doctor.
  • Liver disease: rare cases of liver injury have been reported. Stop and see a doctor if you notice yellowing skin, dark urine or abdominal pain.
  • Medicines for diabetes, blood pressure, immune suppression or sedation: ashwagandha may interact with them. Ask your doctor.
  • Hormone-sensitive prostate cancer: avoid unless your specialist agrees.

Safety beyond about 3 months has not been well studied. If you plan to use it for longer, review it with your doctor.

FAQ

Is it better to take ashwagandha in the morning or at night?

Trials used both. Night suits a wind-down routine and the sleep studies, so it is in the Super Routine Night Pack.

What is the difference between KSM-66 and normal ashwagandha?

KSM-66 is a root-only extract standardised to withanolides, so each dose is consistent. Generic powders vary in strength and may include leaf.

How long does ashwagandha take to work?

Most trials measured results after 8–12 weeks of daily use.
