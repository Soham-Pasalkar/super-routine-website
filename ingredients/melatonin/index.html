Melatonin 3 mg: when to take it and how it works

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 21 Oct 2026

Melatonin is the hormone that tells the body clock it is night. Take it 30–60 minutes before your usual bedtime. In studies it shortened the time to fall asleep in people with delayed sleep timing, and eased jet lag. The Super Routine Night Pack contains 3 mg, a common adult dose. It is not for children or for anyone pregnant or breastfeeding.

How melatonin works

The brain releases melatonin when it gets dark. It acts on MT1 and MT2 receptors in the body's master clock, which lowers alertness and shifts the body towards sleep. Bright screens late at night, irregular schedules and travel across time zones can all delay this signal.

What the research shows

  • Falling asleep: in people with delayed sleep timing, melatonin helped them fall asleep about 20–35 minutes sooner in reviewed studies, especially when combined with a fixed bedtime.
  • Jet lag: studies suggest it reduces jet lag symptoms after flights both east and west.
  • Chronic insomnia: evidence is weaker. Behavioural therapy (CBT-I) is the first-line approach.

When and how to take it

Take the Night sachet 30–60 minutes before your target bedtime, at about the same time every night, and dim screens and lights after taking it. Do not take melatonin in the morning or during the day.

Why 3 mg

3 mg is a widely used adult dose. Higher doses do not reliably work better and are more likely to cause next-day grogginess. Some people do well on less. If you feel drowsy the next morning, take it earlier in the evening and speak to a pharmacist.

Who should not take it

  • Children and teenagers, unless a doctor advises it.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
  • People with epilepsy, or taking blood thinners, sedatives or immunosuppressants: ask your doctor first.
  • People with dementia: guidelines advise against it.
  • Do not drive or operate machinery after taking melatonin.

Common side effects are mild: headache, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness. Long-term safety data are limited.

FAQ

How long before bed should I take melatonin?

30–60 minutes before your usual bedtime, at the same time each night.

Can I take melatonin and magnesium together?

Yes. They work in different ways and are commonly taken together in the evening. Check with your doctor if you take sedatives.

Is 3 mg of melatonin too much?

3 mg is a common adult dose. Higher doses are not reliably better and can cause morning grogginess. Some people need less.

Is melatonin habit-forming?

Melatonin is not considered habit-forming, but long-term safety has not been well studied. Use it as part of a consistent sleep routine.
