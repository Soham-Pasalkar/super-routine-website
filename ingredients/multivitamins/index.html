13 multivitamins at 100% RDA: forms, doses and when to take them

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 29 Oct 2026

The Super Routine Day Pack contains 13 vitamins: A, D3, E, K2, B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B9, B12 and C. Each is dosed at 100% of the RDA (vitamin A at 90%), in forms chosen for absorption, such as methylcobalamin, L-methylfolate, P5P and K2 as MK-7. Take it after breakfast with a meal that contains some fat.

Why 100% RDA

A daily multivitamin is meant to fill the gaps a normal diet leaves, not to replace food or treat a deficiency. Dosing at the RDA covers the daily need while keeping every vitamin far below its upper limit, even when you also eat fortified foods such as atta, salt, oil or milk. Megadose multivitamins add cost and, for vitamins A and B6 in particular, the risk of excess over time.

Why these forms

  • Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) raises blood vitamin D more effectively than D2. See vitamin D3 + K2.
  • Vitamin K2 as MK-7 stays in the blood longer than K1 and pairs with D3 for bone health.
  • Methylcobalamin, L-methylfolate (5-MTHF) and P5P are active forms of B12, folate and B6 that do not need conversion. See vitamin B12.
  • Riboflavin-5′-phosphate is the active form of B2, and nicotinamide is a form of B3 that does not cause the flushing of nicotinic acid.
  • Retinyl palmitate provides preformed vitamin A at 90% of the RDA, leaving room for vitamin A from food.

What the vitamins do together

Together these vitamins contribute to normal energy-yielding metabolism, normal immune function, normal bones and teeth, normal skin and vision, normal nerve function, the formation of red blood cells, the protection of cells from oxidative stress and the reduction of tiredness and fatigue.

Best time to take a multivitamin

After a meal that contains some fat, because vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble. Breakfast is the easiest meal to make a habit, which is why the multivitamins are in the Day Pack, alongside the trace minerals and L-theanine + Panax ginseng.

Who should be careful

  • Warfarin or other vitamin K antagonists: vitamin K2 can reduce their effect. Do not use without your doctor's approval.
  • Pregnancy: use a prenatal supplement chosen with your doctor instead.
  • Other multivitamins or B-complex tablets: do not combine them with the Day Pack.
  • Methotrexate, some anti-seizure medicines and levodopa can interact with folate or B6. Ask your doctor.

FAQ

What are the 13 vitamins in Super Routine?

Vitamins A, D3, E, K2, B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7 (biotin), B9 (folate), B12 and C, each at 100% of the RDA except vitamin A at 90%.

What is the best time to take a multivitamin?

After a meal that contains some fat, usually breakfast, because vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble.

Is 100% RDA enough?

For filling everyday dietary gaps, yes. If a doctor has diagnosed a deficiency, such as low vitamin D or B12, you will usually need a higher treatment dose for a period.

Can I take the multivitamins with other supplements?

Avoid other multivitamins or B-complex products. Most single supplements are fine, but check with your doctor or pharmacist if you take medicines.

Not medical advice. Super Routine is a food supplement, not a medicine, and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Speak to your doctor before use if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, have a medical condition or take any medicine.
