Trace minerals at 100% RDA: zinc, selenium, iodine, copper, manganese, molybdenum and chromium

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 29 Oct 2026

The Super Routine Day Pack contains 7 trace minerals: zinc, selenium, iodine, copper, manganese, molybdenum and chromium, each at 100% of the RDA, mostly in chelated or organic forms. Take them after breakfast. Magnesium is in the Night Pack, so the minerals never compete for absorption.

What trace minerals do

  • Zinc contributes to normal immune function, skin, hair and nails, and fertility. See zinc picolinate.
  • Selenium contributes to normal thyroid function and the protection of cells from oxidative stress.
  • Iodine contributes to the normal production of thyroid hormones and normal cognitive function.
  • Copper contributes to normal connective tissue, iron transport and immune function, and balances zinc intake.
  • Manganese contributes to normal bone formation and energy metabolism.
  • Molybdenum contributes to the normal metabolism of sulphur amino acids.
  • Chromium contributes to normal macronutrient metabolism and the maintenance of normal blood glucose levels.

Why chelated forms

Minerals bound to amino acids or organic acids, such as zinc picolinate, L-selenomethionine and amino acid chelates, are generally well absorbed and gentle on the stomach compared with cheap oxides. Copper is included at 1.7 mg because long-term zinc intake can lower copper levels, and taking the two together keeps them in balance.

Why there is no iron

Iron competes with zinc for absorption, and most men and post-menopausal women do not need extra iron. Women with low iron or anaemia need a dose chosen by their doctor, so we leave iron out rather than guess.

Best time to take trace minerals

After breakfast, as part of the Day Pack. Taking minerals with food reduces the chance of nausea. Magnesium is taken separately in the Night Pack.

Who should be careful

  • Thyroid conditions or thyroid medicine: iodine and selenium affect thyroid hormones. Ask your doctor first.
  • Diabetes medicine: chromium may add to the effect of glucose-lowering medicines. Monitor your blood sugar and tell your doctor.
  • Antibiotics (quinolones, tetracyclines): take them 2 hours before, or 4–6 hours after, mineral supplements.
  • Wilson's disease or other copper disorders: do not take without medical advice.

FAQ

Which trace minerals are in Super Routine?

Zinc, selenium, iodine, copper, manganese, molybdenum and chromium, each at 100% of the RDA.

Why is there no iron in Super Routine?

Iron competes with zinc for absorption and most men and post-menopausal women do not need extra iron. People with low iron should take a dose chosen by their doctor.

Can I take trace minerals and magnesium together?

At normal doses most people can. Super Routine still separates them, with trace minerals in the Day Pack and magnesium in the Night Pack.

Why is copper included with zinc?

Long-term zinc intake can lower copper levels. Including copper at 100% RDA keeps the two in balance.
