What shilajit is

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like resin that seeps from rocks in the Himalayas. It forms over centuries from decomposed plant matter and has been used in Ayurveda for vitality for a very long time. It is rich in fulvic acid, along with compounds called dibenzo-alpha-pyrones and trace minerals.

What the research shows

Human research is limited. In a 2019 trial, recreationally active men took 250 mg or 500 mg of purified shilajit a day for 8 weeks. The higher dose was linked to better retention of muscle strength after a fatiguing exercise test. Other small studies have looked at energy and male hormone levels. These are early findings in small groups, so we describe shilajit as a traditional ingredient that is being studied for energy and stamina, not as a proven treatment.

Shilajit: morning or night?

Traditional use and most brands favour the morning, because shilajit is used for energy and vitality. There is no clinical evidence that one time is better, so consistency matters more. In Super Routine it is taken after breakfast in the Day Pack, with vitamin D3 + K2, B12 and zinc.

Purity: the part that matters most

Raw or poorly processed shilajit can contain lead, arsenic, mercury and cadmium, along with fungal contamination. Our specification for shilajit is no detectable lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium or thallium. Each batch will be tested by an independent laboratory before release, and the results will be published on this site.

Who should avoid it

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women, and children.
  • Anyone with a medical condition, or taking any medicine: ask your doctor first.
  • Stop taking it and see a doctor if you notice any unusual symptoms.

Traditionally in the morning, because it is used for energy and vitality. No study shows one time is better. In Super Routine it is in the morning Day Pack.

How much shilajit should I take?

Human studies have used 250–500 mg a day of purified shilajit. Super Routine contains 250 mg.

Is shilajit safe?

Only purified, lab-tested shilajit should be used, because raw resin can contain heavy metals. Avoid it if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, and speak to your doctor if you have a medical condition or take medicines.
