Night Pack: your evening supplements in one sachet

Written and reviewed by Shubham Pasalkar, FounderUpdated 21 Oct 2026

The Night Pack holds four nutrients for the evening: magnesium glycinate (1000 mg, giving 140 mg elemental magnesium), ashwagandha KSM-66 (300 mg), omega-3 in rTG form (750 mg) and melatonin (3 mg). Take one sachet after dinner or 30–60 minutes before bed.

Why these four go at night

Melatonin only makes sense before bed. It signals to the body clock that it is night, so it has to be taken in the evening. Taken in the morning it can cause daytime drowsiness.

Magnesium glycinate pairs magnesium with glycine, an amino acid, and is gentle on the stomach. Many people take magnesium as part of a wind-down routine, and the glycinate form is less likely to have a laxative effect than oxide or citrate. See our magnesium glycinate guide.

Omega-3 with dinner. Omega-3 can be taken at any time of day. Taking it with the evening meal fits the routine and reduces fishy aftertaste for many people.

Ashwagandha has been studied for stress and sleep, and the evening suits a calmer routine. Trials mostly lasted 8–12 weeks.

How to take it

  • Take one Night sachet after dinner or 30–60 minutes before bed, with water.
  • Do not drive or operate machinery after taking it, because melatonin can make you drowsy.
  • Do not combine with other sleep aids or sedatives without asking your doctor.
  • Not for children, or for anyone pregnant or breastfeeding.

FAQ

Can I take magnesium and melatonin together?

Yes. They work differently: melatonin signals night to the body clock and magnesium supports normal muscle and nerve function. Both are commonly taken in the evening. Check with your doctor if you take sedatives or blood thinners.

Will the Night Pack make me groggy in the morning?

3 mg of melatonin is a common adult dose. Some people, especially older adults, feel drowsy the next morning. If that happens, take the sachet earlier in the evening and speak to a pharmacist.

Is it safe to take every night?

The doses are within commonly studied ranges, but long-term safety data for melatonin and ashwagandha are limited. Most ashwagandha trials lasted up to 3 months. Review your routine with your doctor if you plan to use it for longer.
